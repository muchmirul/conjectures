Grow the table, on a movable canvas

The same idea as chapter 3's page: take the first few people of the sixteen-person, three-colour table and watch the one-colour triangle count stay at zero. Here the people are real objects you can pick up and move.

Try this. Walk the slider up to 16, then press Scatter. The circle was only ever a convenient way to draw the table. Drag people wherever you like: the connections follow them, the colours never change, and the count stays at zero. Safety lives in the pattern of colours, not in the arrangement on screen.

loading the canvas…

The canvas could not load. This page fetches React and the tldraw SDK from a CDN, so it needs a network connection, and some browsers refuse to load modules from a page opened directly as a file.

To run it locally, serve the folder over HTTP instead of double-clicking the file:

cd docs/multicolor-ramsey/tldraw && python3 -m http.server 8000

then open http://localhost:8000/.

Everything in the play pages works offline with no libraries; only this experiment needs the network.

Each colour on its own is a net with no triangle at all, which is why the whole table is safe. The show one colour buttons fade the other two so you can check that claim by eye, the same split the article draws as a still picture.

The count below the canvas is computed from the colouring itself, by the same code as the chapter 3 page, so moving or deleting people on the canvas does not change it. That is the point being made: the colouring is the mathematics, the drawing is just a drawing. Three colours force a triangle at seventeen people, by Greenwood and Gleason in 1955, and that direction is quoted rather than checked here.
